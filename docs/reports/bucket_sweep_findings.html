Indicator Bucket Sweep — Findings & Recommendations

What followed every joint indicator state across eight WTI contract families, and what of it survives honest statistical scrutiny.

13,675,025 cells 8 families 24 indicators · 7 themes 5,015 combinations (k≤4) horizons 1,2,3,5,10,15,20 2013-01-30 → 2026-07-02

The sweep ran clean and the engineering is sound — cutoffs are point-in-time, alignment is verified, nothing leaks. But the headline result is a negative one, and it is the most valuable thing here: the raw t-statistics in this dataset are not interpretable as evidence. Almost all of the apparent significance is an artifact of counting correlated observations as independent. One genuine, weak, economically-plausible relationship survives — and the live scoring engine currently weights it at zero.

Bottom line

Do not trade any cell off its t_stat. Use the sweep as a descriptive instrument — it is excellent at that — and promote only relationships that show a monotone bucket gradient with a coherent horizon term structure, which is a far stronger evidence standard than any single cell's significance.

01The multiple-testing problem is the finding

2,188,959 cells clear |t| > 3 — against roughly 36,900 expected by chance. A 59× excess looks spectacular until you ask what an "observation" is. Two structural dependencies inflate every t-statistic in the study:

  • Overlapping forward windows. At horizon 20, consecutive daily observations share 19 of 20 bars. They are nearly the same measurement counted twenty times.
  • Cross-contract clustering. Cells pool every contract in a family. On a median day, calendars has 90 contracts trading — all driven by the same curve. That is one observation, not ninety.

The mechanism, family by family

If t-inflation is really driven by cross-contract correlation, the excess should scale with how many correlated contracts each family carries. It does — almost monotonically. This is the diagnostic that turns a suspicion into a conclusion:

Read flies differently from everything else

Because a butterfly nets out the level factor, its cells are far closer to independent than any other family's. flies is the one place in this dataset where a t-statistic is worth roughly what it says. Everywhere else, divide by something between 3 and 10 before believing it.

03The one relationship worth your attention

Eight indicator × family pairs are monotone at all seven horizons. Three of them are the same indicator — rsi_div_14, the RSI-vs-price divergence — in three different spread families, all with the same sign, all with large samples.

The directional read is consistent too. At horizon 20, the low bucket is up 48.6–51.9% of the time and the high bucket only 42.7–44.4% — a 5.0–9.2 point spread in directional accuracy that widens smoothly with horizon.

The sign is the opposite of the textbook

rsi_div_14 is positive when momentum is stronger than price action warrants — classically read as a bullish divergence. In this dataset a high reading precedes lower forward moves, consistently, across three families and seven horizons.

Before acting on that, rule out the boring explanation: the feature is built from z-scored changes, so a high reading also tags rows where price recently fell. What looks like "fade the divergence" may just be short-horizon continuation of the move that created it. That is a one-afternoon test, and it decides whether this is a signal or a re-description of momentum.

04What this says about the live scoring engine

Here is the most actionable item in the report. The Opportunity Score already carries an rsi_div_term. Its fitted weights:

The weight search zeroed out the one indicator this sweep identifies as the most stable relationship in the entire dataset. That is not necessarily a contradiction — it is a clue, and the mechanism is visible in the code:

rsi_div_term is scored through _agree_mag(row, cal, "rsi_div_14", _dir_px_range(level)), which returns the magnitude only when the divergence's sign agrees with the direction implied by level-based mean reversion, and 0.0 otherwise.

But the effect this sweep found is unconditional — it shows up on rsi_div_14 alone, with no reference to level. A gate that discards the signal whenever it disagrees with level-reversion will throw away roughly half the information, and the weight search will then quite correctly assign the gutted term near-zero weight. The term may be worthless because of how it is gated, not because the indicator is uninformative.

05Recommendations

Do first Test an ungated rsi_div_term

Add a variant scored on rsi_div_14's own signed percentile — no _dir_px_range(level) gate — and re-run the weight search on quarterly, semestral and yearly. If the ungated form takes meaningful weight where the gated one took zero, the sweep has found a real gap in the scoring engine. This is a contained change with a clear falsifiable outcome.

Do first Settle the divergence sign question

Regress the forward move on rsi_div_14 while controlling for recent price change (mom_5 / mom_10). If the divergence effect vanishes once you control for the move that produced it, this is momentum wearing a different hat and should not become a separate term. Cheap to run, and it gates everything above.

Next Report a clustered t-statistic, not the raw one

The current t_stat column invites exactly the wrong inference, and top_cells.csv ranks on it. Add a t_clustered column that divides by √(contracts_per_date × horizon), and rank on that instead. Keep the raw column for continuity, but the ranking file — the artifact anyone will actually open — should lead with the honest number.

Next Promote on gradient, not on significance

Add a monotonicity screen to the pipeline: for each indicator, require the mean to be ordered across low → mid → high and the bucket spread to grow with horizon. That screen reduced 13.7M cells to eight candidate relationships and surfaced the only one worth investigating. It is a far better filter than any t-threshold, and it is a dozen lines of code.

Then Give flies more attention than its size suggests

It is the least contaminated family in the study — 2 contracts a day, level factor netted out, a 4.2× excess where calendars shows 142×. Statistical work done on flies is worth more per cell than anywhere else in the book, even though it is the smallest family. Consider running it at finer bucket resolution.

Then Replace the cost stubs before any P&L claim

COST_STUB_POINTS is flagged TODO(fase-0) in the source and ranges 0.01–0.03 points. Every "beats cost" mark in this report is measured against a placeholder. The rsi_div_14 spreads (0.27–0.58 pts at h=20) clear it by an order of magnitude, so the conclusion is unlikely to flip — but the margin is unquantified until real bid/offer and slippage go in.

Then Stop before k=4

The 4-way combinations contributed 3,544 of 5,015 combinations and the overwhelming majority of the 13.7M cells — and produced nothing that survived. Every relationship worth reporting came from k=1. Running at --max-k 2 would cut the output roughly 60-fold and lose nothing that this run demonstrated. Deep conditioning here buys narrower cells, not more information.

06What this study cannot tell you

  • It is in-sample by selection. Every input is point-in-time — the cutoffs are expanding quantiles over strictly prior dates, verified — but choosing a cell because it looks good is still fitting. Nothing here has been confirmed on held-out dates.
  • No costs, no sizing, no execution. These are raw point moves measured close-to-close from a next-bar-open anchor. Nothing is netted for spread, slippage or commission.
  • The deflation factors are heuristics, not a formal correction. √(h·m) assumes perfect correlation within a date and across an overlapping window; the truth sits between the raw and fully-deflated columns. A block bootstrap over dates would replace the bracket with a number.
  • Hit rate is direction-agnostic. It counts "price rose", not "the trade won". For a short thesis, read 1 − hit_rate.
  • Bucket boundaries drift. A given raw indicator value can be mid in 2014 and low in 2023. That is correct and deliberate, but it means a cell is not a fixed threshold rule — read <family>_cutoffs.parquet to recover what applied on any date.
